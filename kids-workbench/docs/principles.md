# Principles

Autobahn model: a recommended speed (*Richtgeschwindigkeit*), not a ticket for every limit.
If the system is well set up, users want to use it well.

- **Recommend, don't block.** Show what most people do and the usual risk. The kid can override.
- **Guardrails only for extremes.** Hard stops for the irreversible or dangerous: deleting a project with no backup, flashing the wrong thing to a car ECU, mains-voltage work.
- **Guide and trust.** Keep an eye, mentor, add guides and protection for extremes, otherwise let them do their thing.
- **Transparency over surveillance.** Anything a parent or mentor can see, the kid knows about, ideally because the kid chose to share.
- **Make the good path the easy path.** Auto-snapshots, templates, 10-second checklists.
- **The kid owns it.** Their projects, data, and publishing choices.
- **Failure is information.** A failed print or burnt joint goes in the log as "what I learned", not as an error.
- **Co-design.** Kids help shape the system; a regular "what's working, what's annoying" session.
- **Experiment openly.** Plain-text config the family can edit together; customizing is part of learning.

## Time management stance
- Not time limits. Help with rhythm.
- Weekly rhythm of make / move / rest / share.
- Suggest physical breaks tied to the project ("test the kart for 20 minutes"), not generic stretch reminders.
- Optional daily log written by the kid ("what I made, what I learned"), no scoring.
