# Hardware and workshop

## Device detection ("what did you plug in?")
Recognize a device and offer the right app: camera, MIDI/audio interface, DMX, Arduino/ESP32/Pico, printer, OBD-II dongle, Makey Makey.
Pre-solve Linux pain points once: udev rules, serial permissions, flashing, PipeWire audio setup.

## Physical computing
- Microcontrollers: Arduino, ESP32, Raspberry Pi Pico (MicroPython/CircuitPython).
- Sensors and actuators: motors, servos, LEDs, buttons, temperature, GPS (go-kart).
- Makey Makey (appears as a keyboard, already works).
- Test tools: multimeter reference; later USB oscilloscope or logic analyzer.

## Music and performance
- Audio interfaces and MIDI that work on first plug-in.
- DAW: Ardour, LMMS.
- DMX lighting: QLC+.
- Recording and streaming: OBS.
- Cameras.
- Concert / show mode.

## Fabrication
- 3D printer (OctoPrint / Klipper).
- Possible later: laser cutter, vinyl cutter, CNC.
- Soldering station with fume extraction.
- Wood shop tools (table saw, router, drill press).
- Car tools, jack stands, OBD-II (ELM327 dongle).

## Where the computer lives
Open: fixed workshop station, rolling cart, or travelling laptop.
Considerations:
- Power: battery, portable power, 12V option.
- Network: mobile hotspot or local WiFi mesh for garage/treehouse.
- Dirt: washable keyboard cover, wall mount or stand, protect vents from sawdust and solvent.
- Rugged input: touchscreen or tablet, Bluetooth keyboard, big-button remote.
- Rugged case.
