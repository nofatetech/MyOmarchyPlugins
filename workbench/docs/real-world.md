# Real-world pathways

Inspiration: Acton, Montessori, Waldorf, Sudbury-style approaches. Learning through real work in real context, self-directed, adults as guides. Support that without becoming a curriculum.

## Portfolio as the spine
- The project log (photos, notes, reflections) is the portfolio.
- Exports as PDF or simple website.
- Usable for apprenticeship applications, school transfer, maker-club entry.

## Skills map
- Personal skill tree instead of grades: soldering, CAD, welding, recording, coding, woodwork, car repair.
- Evidence attached to each node ("here's the thing I made").
- Mentors can vouch.

## Internships and apprenticeships
- List of local workshops, garages, studios, makerspaces the family knows or finds, with contact and status notes.
- "Ask for a visit" email template in the kid's own voice.
- In Germany: connects to Schuelerpraktikum and Ausbildung paths (confirm context, see open questions).

## Real commissions
- Small paid or useful jobs: fix a neighbor's bike, make a sign, record a friend's band.
- Track quote, materials, deadline, result. Teaches money, deadlines, customers.

## Public contribution
- Open-source projects, Thingiverse uploads, itch.io games, repair videos.
- Real feedback from real people.

## Rhythms from those pedagogies
- Long uninterrupted work blocks.
- Time for reflection.
- Seasonal themes.
- Weekly "what do I want to learn" prompt.
